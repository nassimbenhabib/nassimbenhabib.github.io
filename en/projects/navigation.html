UAVIA

Autonomous drone navigation

From motion planning to constrained control, precision landing and safety behaviors.

C++ · MPC · QP · Dijkstra · RTK / IMU
The problemMy contributionTechnical approachValidation

The problem

A navigation system must translate mission objectives into executable motion while respecting obstacles, flight limits and operational transitions. My work at UAVIA covered several connected functions of this autonomy stack.

My contribution

  • Implemented a constrained MPC controller in C++, formulated as a quadratic program and solved with qpOASES.
  • Connected global Dijkstra planning, local safety corridors and motion control.
  • Designed precision-landing logic based on RTK and IMU data, including an evolving capture zone, phased descent and contact detection.
  • Implemented geofencing and fallback behaviors accounting for wind, position uncertainty and projected falling trajectories.
  • Developed terrain-following and mission-specific navigation for gas-emission detection and coverage.

Technical approach

Planning and control

Global planning structures the route. Local corridors define admissible space, while predictive control accounts for motion limits such as velocity, acceleration, braking and yaw.

Landing and operational logic

Landing is organized as a sequence of approach and descent phases with explicit transition conditions. Sensor data and contact detection inform the progression.

Integration

C++ development using Eigen, Boost and qpOASES, coupled with autonomy services and flight-platform interfaces.

Validation

Simulation, telemetry analysis, parameter tuning and flight testing were used to assess the implemented behavior. The emphasis is on integrating planning, control and operational logic, then assessing their behavior on the flight platform.
