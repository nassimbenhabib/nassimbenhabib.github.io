INRIA BORDEAUX

Physical human–robot assistance

Modeling, simulation and constrained assistance control for an industrial manual task.

Dynamics · Identification · QP · MPC · Human–robot interaction
The problemMy contributionTechnical approachValidation

The problem

The doctoral research addresses cobotic assistance for a spindle-moulding task, where an operator manipulates a workpiece in interaction with a machine tool. The aim is to model these physical interactions and develop assistance compatible with the task constraints.

My contribution

  • Built a dynamic model of human–robot–environment interaction.
  • Formulated constrained inverse dynamics and force estimation through quadratic optimization.
  • Developed a numerical simulator and an instrumented robotic test platform.
  • Identified model parameters from measured trajectories and forces.
  • Implemented assistance using predictive control, quadratic optimization and physical constraints.

Technical approach

Model and identification

Measured motion and forces support parameter identification. The model is assessed using trials separate from those used for identification.

Physical constraints

Contact conditions and physical limits are represented in the optimization problem, connecting assistance objectives to feasible robot actions.

Simulation and experimental platform

The numerical simulator and instrumented platform provide complementary means to compare predicted and measured behavior under nominal and perturbed conditions.

Validation

The work combines simulation and experimental validation, with comparisons of motion and force measurements. The ICRA 2020 paper documents simulation and experimental validation for the carpentry task; the broader doctoral work also includes subsequent modeling and assistance developments.
