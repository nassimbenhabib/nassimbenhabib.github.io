INRIA / DOCTORAL RESEARCH

Quantifying manual expertise

A multidimensional view of task performance, safety-related actions and physical demands.

Experimental protocol · Motion & force analysis · Manual expertise
The problemMy contributionTechnical approachValidation

The problem

Similar task performance can result from different movement and effort strategies. This work examines how manual expertise is expressed in an instrumented industrial task, beyond a single measure of execution quality.

My contribution

  • Analyzed an experimental protocol involving 43 participants across four experience levels.
  • Constructed six criteria covering motion regularity, transverse guidance, holding actions, perturbation response, manual effort and posture.
  • Examined relationships between criteria and differences between participant profiles.
  • Studied trial variability, recurrence of individual differences and changes within the session.

Technical approach

Multidimensional criteria

Performance, safety-related behavior and physical demands are considered together. They describe complementary aspects of task execution rather than a single global expertise score.

Comparisons and interpretation

Group effects and experimental conditions are considered in the analysis. Statistical associations are distinguished from physical interpretation and causal claims.

Within-session changes

Comparisons between early and late trials help describe changes in individual profiles. A change is not automatically interpreted as learning or improvement.

Validation

The analyses form part of the doctoral manuscript in progress. The study combines a multidimensional description of task execution with analysis of inter-individual differences and within-session changes.
